✨ What GeoINT Actually Does

Most location tools throw a map pin at you and hope for the best. GeoINT works differently. It thinks first, then checks itself.

When you hand it an image, it starts by writing down its first honest impression of the scene — the kind of gut call a strong GeoGuessr player makes in the first ten seconds. Sun angle, road markings, the shape of the hills, the language on a sign, the colour of the soil, the style of the power poles. That first read is saved in writing.

Then it goes to work. It searches the web, compares details, follows leads and uses local tools to narrow things down. It is allowed to prove its first impression right, sharpen it to a tighter area, or throw it out completely when the evidence says otherwise. When the evidence is good enough, it can land on a specific street, or even the exact camera position that took the shot.

The result is not just an address. You get a full written report showing the reasoning, the clues that mattered, and a clear note about how confident the answer really is.

🧭 How the Process Works

Every case follows the same disciplined path, which is what makes the answers trustworthy:

  1. The first read. GeoINT studies the scene and writes an initial location guess with its reasons.
  2. The research. Details from the image are checked against real-world sources, maps and reference material.
  3. The proof. Local tools test the remaining candidates. A match either holds up or it falls apart.
  4. The report. Everything is written down with the evidence, so you can follow the logic yourself.

This is the difference between a lucky guess and an investigation. Even when the final answer is uncertain, you can see exactly which clues were strong and which ones were shaky.

🗂️ Every Photo Gets Its Own Archive

Nothing gets lost. Each image you bring to GeoINT is filed away in its own timestamped folder. Inside that folder you will find:

  • A written report describing the location and how it was reached.
  • Images collected along the way that support the conclusion.
  • A scoreboard entry, filled in once the true location is confirmed.

Over time this becomes your own personal collection of solved cases — handy for checking back, comparing notes, or seeing how much sharper your instincts have become.

🏔️ Skyline to Place: The New Trick

The newest addition handles one of the hardest situations in geolocation: a photo where the only real clue is a line of hills or mountains on the horizon.

You give GeoINT the photo and tell it roughly which region those mountains belong to. It then ranks every possible place in that region by how well the shape of the terrain there matches the shape in your picture. To keep itself honest, it also tests decoy skylines as a control, so a match has to actually mean something.

Each ranked place arrives with a plain-language honesty rating — solid, check, or weak — so you know whether you are looking at a strong lead or just a possibility. It is a genuinely useful way to turn an empty horizon into a shortlist of real places.

💡 Tips for Better Results

  • Start with the fullest image you have. A full-resolution original beats a cropped, compressed copy almost every time.
  • Give it context when you can. If you already know the country or region, say so. GeoINT will still check everything, but it can focus faster.
  • Send several images of the same place. Two angles of the same street corner often crack a case that a single shot cannot.
  • Read the confidence rating. "Solid" means the evidence lines up. "Weak" means it is a lead, not an answer.
  • Keep the archive. Old reports are useful references when a similar scene shows up again.
